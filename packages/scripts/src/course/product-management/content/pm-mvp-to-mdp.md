Three months after the rent tool's lean launch, the team has a spreadsheet of 214 feature requests and a founder asking when "the MDP" ships. The instructor's version of this step is a ride-sharing app whose MVP books rides and notifies drivers. Feedback says users want fare estimates, the design feels too basic, and navigation is slow at peak times. So the MDP improves peak performance, adds fare estimates and trip history, brings in a sleek design with a modern color scheme, and adds maps for live tracking. Every comment became a feature. Before adding anything, read what the MVP measured.

## Four readings

The instructor lists the MVP's metrics: adoption rate, engagement with the core feature, retention, and qualitative feedback. For the rent tool's launch to notebook owners:

```text
Adoption rate = new users / target audience reached x 100
  Saw the offer, with its price     640 owners
  Set up at least one tenant        160
  Adoption                          160 / 640 x 100 = 25%

Core-feature engagement, month 1
  Sent reminders                    128 of 160 = 80%
  Updated tenant records             24 of 160 = 15%

Retention, same 160 owners
  Paid for month 3                   88 of 160 = 55%
  Revenue                            88 x ₹299 = ₹26,312 a month
```

Each line answers a different question and has a trap.

**Adoption** says whether the offer interests the people who see it. The video's formula divides by the target audience size, but its example divides by the people you reached. Divided by all 1,200 Roost owners, the same 160 reads as 13%. Divide by the people who saw the offer, and say so.

**Engagement** says what the product is. Reminders are the product; tenant records, used by 15%, are a feature few wanted. The instructor adds that good PMs delete features nobody uses, and the MVP is the cheapest moment to do it.

**Retention** says whether the value lasts, and it means something only within a group that started together. The video's version, returning users over all users, moves whenever signups do: a slow month for new owners makes retention look better with nobody staying longer.

**Qualitative feedback** explains the numbers. And one number is a warning: ₹26,312 a month against the product mix lesson's forecast of ₹89,700. The next version has to raise retention and reach, not only please the 88.

## Fix, add, leave out

An MDP is not the sum of all requests. It is the MVP's core job done well enough that the people it serves stay. For the MDP, the instructor moves the scoreboard to satisfaction, churn and revenue per user, and those only improve when the core holds. Sort requests by what they do for that job:

- **Fix** whatever breaks the core job, even when those it drove away can no longer ask.
- **Add** what the users who stayed need to finish the job.
- **Leave out** requests for other jobs, however loud, and polish with no evidence behind it.

Keep the instructor's last step, user testing the new features: every addition is a new hypothesis.

## Predict, then verify

The team has six engineer-weeks this quarter. The largest requests:

| Request                               | Asked by | Mostly from         | Weeks |
| ------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------- | ----- |
| Tick off UPI payments automatically   | 51       | Owners still paying | 4     |
| A modern dashboard redesign           | 44       | Both groups         | 3     |
| Split electricity bills among tenants | 38       | Owners who left     | 3     |
| Reminders not arriving                | 20       | Owners who left     | 1     |

The founder wants the redesign first, because a desirable product has to look good. What goes into the six weeks?

Answer: The reminder fix and automatic UPI matching, five weeks, with the sixth spent testing both with owners. Reminders that never arrive break the core job, and the 20 owners who left over them are evidence of a leak, not of a different need. UPI matching finishes the job, since an owner wants rent collected and accounted for, not just requested, and the owners asking are the ones paying Roost. Bill splitting is a real pain but a different job, raised mostly by owners who left for other reasons. Nothing shows the redesign changes who stays, so it waits for a test of its own. The principle: an MDP is built from what the users who stayed need to finish the core job, plus fixes for whatever broke it, not from the loudest request or the prettiest one.
