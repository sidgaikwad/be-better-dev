Each month about 8,000 students enquire about a room on Roost and 4,000 book. Roost's persona came from student interviews only, so the PM phoned 30 families whose child had enquired and not booked. 19 gave a parent's reason. One father had been sent a link to a ₹9,000-a-month room in a city he had never seen, with nothing saying who had checked it. The persona had one person in it. The purchase had two.

## Buyer, user, customer

The course's instructor separates three terms: a **user persona** for whoever uses the product day to day, a **buyer persona** for whoever makes the purchase decision, and a **customer persona** covering both. Sometimes they are one person, like whoever downloads and pays for a fitness app. In business software they usually split: the video's team lead buys a team management tool that developers and designers use every day.

The video tells PMs to focus on the user, and to add the buyer when the purchase decides whether users get access at all. Whenever someone else pays, it does.

The term buyer persona is most associated with Adele Revella, whose _Buyer Personas_ (2015) builds them from interviews with recent buyers. A user persona asks what someone is trying to do; hers asks why and how they bought, in five "rings of buying insight". For Roost:

| Ring                | Ananya, the user              | Her father, the buyer                        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Priority initiative | Term starts in three weeks    | His daughter asks him to pay                 |
| Success factors     | Near campus, booked fast      | She is safe; the cost is final               |
| Perceived barriers  | Photos may be fake            | Fraud; a city he cannot visit                |
| Decision criteria   | Rent, distance, photos        | Who checked it, total cost, owner's identity |
| Buyer's journey     | 40 listings, a shortlist of 4 | Two WhatsApp links, then pays                |

The video's own example, "Tech-savvy Tina", lists goals and a challenge, but not what triggers her purchase or what makes her walk away.

## Three roles in a business sale

The MEDDIC sales framework, created at PTC in 1996 by Dick Dunkel with Jack Napoli, names the **economic buyer**, who has final authority to spend, and the **champion**, an insider who pushes the deal forward.

In the video's team tool, the team lead is the economic buyer and needs a case in money or risk: fewer missed deadlines at a price the budget allows. A senior developer who trialed it is the champion and needs a demo and an answer for the security review. The developers, the users, need their daily work to get faster. A tool the users love that the buyer cannot justify is never bought. One the buyer loves that the users avoid is bought once and cancelled at renewal.

## Designing for both

Build the product for the user, build the purchase for the buyer, and never pay for the buyer's approval with control over the user. Roost's answer to the 19 families is a page the student sends to a parent: the verification report with its visit date, dated photos, every charge, the owner's verified identity, a pay button and a receipt. Ananya's app is unchanged; her father's criteria are met at the moment he decides.

## Predict, then verify

A survey of 500 paying parents finds 62% would prefer a PG that sends them a weekly attendance report. Twenty PGs in Pune already send one, and first-years shortlist them 45% less often than similar PGs nearby. In about 80% of bookings the student builds the shortlist and a parent approves one room. Do you make the report a Roost feature?

Answer: No. The student builds the shortlist in 80% of bookings, so a feature that makes students skip a listing removes it before most parents see it. The parents' 62% is a stated preference about a hypothetical, the kind of answer the lesson on why "would you use this?" fails taught you to discount; the students' 45% is behavior. The survey does reveal the parent's success factor, knowing the child is safe. Serve that at the purchase: the PG's gate timings, security staff and an emergency contact, verified and shown on the parent page. The principle: meet the buyer's criteria where the buyer decides, and never make the user's product the buyer's surveillance tool. When the user picks, a feature the user rejects never reaches the buyer.
