A free listing portal lets owners post rooms themselves and charges them nothing. Roost pays ₹600 to visit each property before listing it, then charges owners 8% of the first month's rent. On price Roost loses every comparison, yet it books about 4,000 rooms a month. Michael Porter's generic strategies explain why.

## Three ways to beat the average

In _Competitive Strategy_ (1980), Porter argued that a company earns more than its industry's average in one of three ways: the lowest costs, something buyers value enough to pay more for, or a narrow target served better than broad rivals can. In _Competitive Advantage_ (1985) he split focus in two, giving a grid of advantage against the width of the target.

| Target | Lower cost               | Differentiation                |
| ------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Broad  | Cost leadership: Walmart | Differentiation: Apple         |
| Narrow | Cost focus: Aldi         | Differentiation focus: Ferrari |

The examples are the video's, which adds Jio as a cost leader, plus Tesla and Rolls-Royce.

Walmart fits: buying power and cheap distribution keep its costs low. Jio is harder, and shows Porter's meaning. It launched in September 2016 with free data and calls until March 2017, and reached 100 million subscribers within 170 days. Free is a price. Cost leadership is a cost position: the lowest cost to serve a customer, which lets you price low and still earn. Whether Jio was a cost leader or a well-funded entrant pricing below cost depends on its costs, which its price cannot reveal.

Differentiation is the mirror image: something buyers value, sold at a premium larger than it costs you to provide. Focus is a choice of scope: serve one segment so well that broad competitors, who must serve everyone else too, cannot match you there, as Aldi does with a small, mostly own-brand range for shoppers who put price first.

## Where Roost sits

Roost's target is narrow: students near campus, above all first-years moving to a new city. Its advantage is not cost; the portal spends almost nothing per listing. What Roost sells owners is a filled bed, booked by families who will not pay for a room nobody has seen. An empty bed costs an owner ₹9,000 / 30 = ₹300 a day, so the ₹720 fee pays for itself if a Roost booking fills the bed 720 / 300 = 2.4 days sooner than the owner's next channel. That is differentiation focus. The strategy kernel lesson's guiding policy, compete on the truth of each listing, not on discounts, says the same.

## Stuck in the middle

Porter warned that a firm chasing all three usually gets none: costs above the cost leader's, and a product less special than the differentiator's. The instructor notes that most companies want the best product at the lowest cost, and rarely get it. The bad strategy lesson struck "most affordable" from the founder's slide for this reason.

It is also Porter's most disputed claim. Charles Hill (1988) argued that differentiation can raise volume enough to lower unit costs, so the two can combine, and Porter's 1985 book allowed exceptions, such as a firm that pioneers a major innovation. Software makes this common, since one more customer costs little. What survives is the discipline: know which advantage you pay for, and refuse spending that erodes it.

## Predict, then verify

The kernel ruled out unverified listings, but the founder has a number. Roost Basic: owners list themselves without a visit, at 4% commission instead of 8%, bringing an expected 1,000 extra bookings a year. Roost makes about 48,000 bookings a year, all on visited rooms, and "parents won't pay until someone has seen the room" came up in 9 of 14 student interviews. Do you launch Basic?

Answer: No. Basic earns 1,000 × 4% × ₹9,000 = 1,000 × ₹360 = ₹360,000 a year, the commission on ₹360,000 / ₹720 = 500 visited-room bookings, about 1% of 48,000. If Basic costs Roost more than one booking in a hundred, it loses money, and it will: it breaks the promise parents rely on, that every room on Roost was visited, and each student who books Basic instead of a visited room halves that booking's commission. Basic also competes with the free portal on cost, where Roost has no edge. This is stuck in the middle in miniature. The principle: know which advantage you pay for, and reject additions that dilute it.
