On March 21, 2016, Apple announced the iPhone SE at $399. Six months earlier the iPhone 6s had launched at $649, and the SE used the same A9 chip in a smaller, older body. That year's annual report said iPhone average selling prices had fallen, partly because of the mix of models sold, including the SE. Apple had built a cheaper phone that some buyers would choose instead of a dearer one. That is cannibalization, done on purpose.

The course's instructor says Apple made the SE for markets such as India. It was a global product, first sold in 13 countries and territories including the US, China and the UK, though a cheaper iPhone matters most in price-sensitive markets like India.

## Extension or modification

There are two ways to change a line, and they are different decisions:

- A **line extension** adds a variant beside what exists: a new flavor, size, price point or segment. Diet Coke, in 1982, was the first new brand to carry the Coca-Cola trademark; Coca-Cola Zero followed in 2005. The original stayed on sale.
- A **product modification** changes the existing product, and the new version replaces the old. Kotler's textbooks name three kinds: quality (durability, reliability, speed), features (new capabilities), and style (how it looks). The video lists four, splitting functional changes from feature upgrades; the line between those two is thin.

The test is simple: an extension adds a choice, and a modification replaces one.

## The cannibalization test

An extension's sales come from two places: people who were not buying before, and people who would have bought the original. Suppose Roost adds Sharing, beds in triple-sharing rooms at an average of ₹5,000 a month, with the same 8% commission:

```text
Forecast Sharing bookings a season        2,000
Commission per Sharing booking   8% × ₹5,000 = ₹400
Commission from Sharing          2,000 × ₹400 = ₹800,000

If 40% would have booked a ₹9,000 room instead:
Bookings cannibalized            2,000 × 0.40 = 800
Commission lost                  800 × ₹720   = ₹576,000
Net gain                         ₹800,000 - ₹576,000 = ₹224,000

Break-even cannibalization       ₹400 / ₹720  = 55.6%
```

Above 55.6%, Sharing loses money even before counting the visits to verify its new properties. The break-even ratio, the new product's margin divided by the old one's, is the number to know before any cheaper variant. For a dearer variant the sign flips, and cannibalization helps.

## When an extension is worth it

The instructor is openly skeptical: in his view most extensions and modifications are more gimmick than strategy, a way to stay in the news. He is right about many of them. Each variant adds stock, testing and support, and a shelf of near-identical versions makes choosing harder. An extension earns its place when three things hold:

1. It serves a need the original cannot meet without getting worse for its own buyers. SE buyers wanted a small, cheap phone; 6s buyers did not.
2. Its net gain survives a realistic cannibalization rate, measured from behavior rather than asked in a survey.
3. It does not blur what the original promises.

## Predict, then verify

Students complain that some listing photos are out of date. Two proposals reach you. One is to re-verify and re-photograph every property once a year: 1,200 visits at about ₹600 each, ₹720,000 a year. The other is Verified Plus, an extension for owners: fresh photos every year for ₹2,000 a season. With 300 owners buying, it earns ₹600,000 against 300 × ₹600 = ₹180,000 of visits. The first costs money and the second makes it. Which do you choose?

Answer: Re-verify everything. The complaint is about Roost's core promise, that a listing shows the room as it really is. A paid Plus badge tells every student that the other 900 properties are checked less carefully, weakening the one thing Roost sells. The ₹720,000 is about 2% of a year's commission (48,000 bookings × ₹720 = ₹34,560,000), a fair price for keeping that promise true. The principle: when the problem sits in the core, modify the core. Extensions are for different needs, never for selling a fix to the base product.
