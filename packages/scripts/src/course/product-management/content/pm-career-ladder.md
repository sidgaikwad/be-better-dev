A PM asks for promotion to senior PM with a strong year behind her: 14 features shipped, every one on time, no incidents. Her manager says no. The request-to-booking rate she was meant to move sat at 50% all year, and every one of the 14 features came from someone else's list. She did the job she was hired into very well. Senior is a different job.

## The rungs

Titles vary, but most ladders share these rungs, and what changes between them is the size of the problem you are handed and how much of it is already defined.

| Rung                      | Owns                                    | Horizon              |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Associate PM              | A well-scoped feature, problem given    | Weeks                |
| PM                        | A feature area and its goal, goal given | A quarter            |
| Senior PM                 | An outcome area, and finds its problems | Six to twelve months |
| Group or principal PM     | Several teams' strategy                 | A year or more       |
| Director                  | A product line, and the PMs on it       | One to two years     |
| VP, chief product officer | The portfolio and the product org       | Several years        |

Four things grow as you climb. **Scope**: a feature, then an area, then a product line, then the company's portfolio. **Ambiguity**: an associate PM is told the problem; a senior PM is told the outcome and finds the problems; a VP is told the company's situation and decides which outcomes matter. **People**: at group PM the ladder usually forks. A group PM manages PMs; a principal PM stays an individual contributor and takes the hardest problem that crosses teams. **Time horizon**: from this sprint to the next several years.

Ravi Mehta, a former chief product officer at Tinder, published a Product Competency Toolkit in 2020 that makes the shift concrete. It names 12 competencies in four areas: product execution (feature specification, product delivery, quality assurance), customer insight (fluency with data, voice of the customer, UX design), product strategy (business outcome ownership, vision and roadmapping, strategic impact) and influencing people (stakeholder management, team leadership, managing up). His point is about weighting: execution decides early careers, and strategy and influence decide growth later. The PM in the opening excelled at the first area and showed nothing in the third. The promotion case she needed was one outcome she chose, argued for, and moved.

## Where the ladder misleads

Titles do not travel. A "Head of Product" at a 20-person startup may be the only PM, doing an associate's scope with a VP's title, and a senior PM at a large company may own more than a startup's whole product. Compare scope and ambiguity, not titles, when you judge an offer or a peer.

The hardest step is the fork. Moving from PM to group PM means your output stops being your decisions and becomes the quality of other PMs' decisions. The common failure is the manager who rewrites a PM's spec the night before review: the spec improves, the PM learns to wait for the rewrite, and the manager becomes the team's bottleneck. If you do not want to spend your days hiring, coaching and reviewing, the principal track exists for you. Ask whether a company has one before you join, because where it does not, the only way up is through management.

## Predict, then verify

Roost has grown to three PMs, and you manage them as group PM. The student PM's parent page, a key result for the season, is two weeks behind, and admissions open in four weeks. You could write the remaining spec yourself in three days. The student PM would need about eight days, with review, and has never had to cut scope under pressure. What do you do?

Answer: do not write it. Sit with the student PM for an hour, ask what the parent page must do on day one for a parent to pay (see the room is real, see every charge), and have them cut the rest from the first release. With scope cut, eight days fits inside four weeks with room for testing. Writing it yourself saves five days once and costs you every later season, because the PM learns that deadlines end with you taking over. At the manager rung, the product you ship is a team that makes good calls without you, so the time goes into the decision, not into the document.
