In one week in March, three requests reach Roost's PM. A food-delivery company wants its menu inside the app for students already living in Roost PGs. The growth lead wants listings for young professionals. The designer wants a roommate-matching feed. Each has a sponsor and a plausible number, and none can be ranked until something upstream says what Roost is for.

The video's instructor puts it plainly: once the product vision is written down, every later decision to add to the product or cut from it has to align with it.

## What a vision is

Marty Cagan defines the product vision as the future you are trying to create, typically 2 to 5 years out, or 5 to 10 for hardware (SVPG, "Vision vs. Strategy", 2016). It is not a spec but a persuasive picture that makes people want to build it. Strategy is how you get there while keeping the business alive.

Three properties separate a vision from its look-alikes:

- **It describes the customer's world, not your size.** "India's biggest student housing platform" is about Roost. A vision is about the student.
- **It is out of reach of any single release**, yet concrete enough that a new engineer can picture it.
- **It rules things out.** A vision compatible with every request is decoration.

Roost's vision:

```text
Every student can move into a room they have never seen,
and find it exactly as promised.
```

A student in Guwahati picks a PG near her Pune college from her phone. Her father pays from a link without sending anyone to check. On move-in day the room, the rent and the rules match the app.

Held against the opening week, it rules out flats for professionals, who are not students, and asks the roommate feed to show how it helps a room match its promise.

## The vision document

The video gives a ten-part template for a product vision document, the reference teams align on through the product's life: introduction, vision statement, target users, problem statement, unique value proposition, goals, market landscape, key features, strategic fit with the company's mission, and a roadmap overview.

The instructor notes that it reads like a business plan without the finance. Fair, with a warning: the goals, features and roadmap go stale within months, while the vision, users, problem and value should hold for years. Put the durable parts first and date the rest. Roost's durable middle:

```text
Target users   First-year students moving to a new city,
               and the parents who pay
Problem        A family commits ₹27,000 upfront to a room
               nobody in it has seen
Unique value   Every listing visited by Roost's team, every
               charge recorded at the visit
```

## Notion's first step

The video offers Notion's early vision as an all-in-one workspace for docs, tasks and ideas. The public wording in March 2018, when Notion 2.0 launched on Product Hunt, was "The all-in-one workspace" for notes, tasks, wikis and databases. Notion's own About page calls that workspace "a first step". The larger aim, which co-founder Ivan Zhao described to Sequoia in 2022, is software as building blocks anyone can assemble into their own tools.

"All-in-one workspace" describes today's product, which makes it closer to a tagline. The building-block idea is the vision, and it explains decisions a tagline cannot, such as making every database row a page built from the same blocks, instead of shipping a better notes app.

## Predict, then verify

The food-delivery partner projects 20,000 orders a month from students in Roost-listed PGs, paying Roost ₹50 an order: ₹1,000,000 a month, over a third of Roost's ₹2,880,000 monthly commission (4,000 bookings × ₹720). Building it takes two of the five engineers from April to August. Does it fit the vision, and do you take it?

Answer: It does not fit, and you decline it this year. The vision is about moving into a room you can trust; food delivery serves students after they have moved in, a different job. Two of five engineers is 40% of the team during the only months when trust work can change bookings. The revenue is weaker than it looks, too: it assumes students order food through a housing app they stop opening once the search ends. If Roost wants a student-life business, it should change the vision openly rather than drift there one tie-up at a time. The principle: a vision is a filter, and a lucrative request that fails it is the test of whether you have one.
