"How big is the market for a student housing marketplace in Pune?" One candidate says ₹13.5 billion. Another says ₹57.6 million. Both did the arithmetic correctly. They sized different things, and only one of them sized the thing the question asked about.

## TAM, SAM, SOM

Market sizing uses three nested numbers:

- **TAM**, total addressable market: the revenue if you had every customer.
- **SAM**, serviceable addressable market: the part your product and channels can actually reach.
- **SOM**, serviceable obtainable market: the part you can realistically win in the next few years.

There are two ways to get the first number. **Top-down** starts from a large known figure and slices it. **Bottom-up** builds from units: customers, times how often they buy, times what you earn each time. Investors and interviewers trust bottom-up more, because each input can be checked, and the strongest answers do both and compare.

## Pune, bottom-up

Every line is an assumption you say out loud. None is a published statistic.

```text
First-year intake, Pune colleges (assume)           125,000
From outside Pune, 50%                               62,500
Without a college hostel seat, 80%                   50,000 new renters a year
Average years a student rents                         3
Students renting at once = 50,000 x 3               150,000
Returning renters = 150,000 - 50,000                100,000
Returning renters who change rooms, 30%              30,000
Room bookings a year = 50,000 + 30,000               80,000
```

The 50,000 matches Roost's own estimate from the product lifecycle lesson, which is a useful first check.

## Pune, top-down

```text
Students in higher education, Pune area (assume)    500,000
Living away from family in paid rooms, 30%          150,000
Average stay in one room, 2 years
Room bookings a year = 150,000 / 2                   75,000
```

The two land within 7% of each other (75,000 against 80,000). They share no inputs: one reaches 150,000 renters through intake and years rented, the other through enrollment and a share. That independence is what makes the agreement mean something.

## From bookings to rupees

```text
Rent spend = 150,000 x ₹9,000 x 10 months         ₹13,500,000,000
TAM = 80,000 bookings x ₹720 commission                ₹57,600,000
SAM = PG and hostel rooms Roost can verify,
      70% of bookings = 56,000 x ₹720                   ₹40,320,000
Today = Roost's 15,000 Pune bookings x ₹720             ₹10,800,000
```

The ₹13.5 billion is what students pay landlords. Roost earns 8% of one month's rent per booking, ₹720, so its market is the commission pool, not the rent. Featured listings add owner revenue on top, but not enough to change the order of magnitude.

## Sanity checks

1. **Does a ratio look human?** 80,000 bookings for 150,000 renters is about one booking per renter every two years. Plausible.
2. **Which assumption moves the answer most?** Years rented. At 2 years instead of 3, renters fall to 100,000, movers to 15,000, and bookings to 65,000, a 19% drop. Say that in the interview; it shows you know where your estimate is weakest.
3. **Does the share make sense?** Roost's 15,000 bookings are 19% of all 80,000, 27% of the 56,000 it can serve, and 30% measured against the 50,000 newcomers, the ratio the product lifecycle lesson tracked. None of these is wrong. A share without its denominator is.

In an estimation interview, the number is barely graded. Structure, stated assumptions, and the checks are.

## Predict, then verify

Roost's founder drafts a fundraising slide: "Pune student housing is a ₹13.5 billion market. Capturing 1% means ₹135 million a year." She wants the same framing for Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Does the slide go out?

Answer: no. At ₹720 a booking, ₹135 million would take 187,500 bookings a year, more than twice the 80,000 bookings Pune has in total and 2.3 times the whole commission TAM of ₹57.6 million. Anyone checking bottom-up finds this in a minute, and it discredits the honest numbers on the next slide. Replace it with TAM ₹57.6 million, SAM ₹40.3 million, and today's ₹10.8 million, a 27% share of what Roost can serve. The rent figure belongs on a slide only if Roost moves into the rent flow itself, as the instalments feature partly does. The principle: size the market in the unit you get paid in.
