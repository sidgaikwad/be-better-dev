Roost's strategy offsite ends with a photo of a whiteboard: a SWOT with 43 sticky notes. Six months later, nobody can name a decision it changed. That is normal. In a 1997 paper, Terry Hill and Roy Westbrook reported on 50 companies in a UK government manufacturing scheme; over 20 had run a SWOT. The lists averaged more than 40 factors, written in general terms, never prioritized and never checked. Worst of all, "no-one subsequently used the outputs" in the later stages of strategy.

## The line that matters

SWOT sorts factors across one line. Strengths and weaknesses are internal: things the company has or does, and can change. Opportunities and threats are external: things in the market it can only respond to. The video's instructor draws the same line.

Most mis-sorted items fail on that line. "Demand is seasonal" is external, a fact of the academic calendar. "54% of our yearly commission lands in 13 weeks while salaries are paid monthly" is internal, a result of Roost's revenue model, and only that version points at something Roost can change. "Launch in Chennai" is not an opportunity at all but an action, which comes after the analysis.

The video's Netflix versus Prime Video example shows the other problem. It lists Prime Video's place in Amazon's ecosystem as a strength, and being seen as secondary to Amazon's shopping business as a weakness: one fact, two boxes. Nothing in the grid says which reading wins, so a SWOT alone cannot produce a decision.

## Evidence first, then pairs

A SWOT worth keeping has two or three items per box, each with its evidence. Roost's:

```text
Strengths
  S1  Every room is visited before listing. 9 of 14 interviews: parents won't pay unseen.
  S2  First-years moving cities: 30% of monthly active students, 58% of bookings.
Weaknesses
  W1  54% of yearly commission lands in 13 weeks; salaries are monthly.
  W2  Paid Instagram ads cost ₹900 per booking against ₹720 earned.
Opportunities
  O1  Colleges want a safe housing list for new students. Pilot: one college emailed
      ours to 1,200 incoming first-years, and 150 booked through it.
  O2  Interns need rooms for 2 to 6 months, all year. (Unmeasured.)
Threats
  T1  Brokers and WhatsApp groups take 65% of Pune first-years who search but do not book.
  T2  Owners can finish a deal off the platform and pay no commission.
```

Heinz Weihrich's TOWS matrix (_Long Range Planning_, 1982) turns the four lists into strategies by pairing internal with external:

- **SO**, a strength seizes an opportunity: become the visited-room list colleges send with admission letters (S1 with O1).
- **ST**, a strength blunts a threat: pay brokers a referral fee to book visited rooms for their clients, so the person a family already trusts sells a room Roost has seen (S1 with T1).
- **WO**, an opportunity fixes a weakness: short stays for interns in the quiet months (O2 with W1).
- **WT**, reduce both: stop buying clicks that brokers then convert (W2 with T1).

Weihrich's contribution is the pairing: the factors were never new, but matching them makes each point at an action. The grid still yields four candidates, and choosing among them is the diagnosis and guiding policy of the strategy kernel lesson. A SWOT is raw material for a diagnosis, not a strategy.

## Predict, then verify

The team can fund one TOWS strategy this quarter. SO: five more colleges have asked for the list, with 5,000 incoming first-years between them; at the pilot's rate of 150 / 1,200 = 12.5%, that is 625 bookings, worth 625 × ₹720 = ₹450,000. WO: the founder estimates 2,000 intern bookings in the quiet months, worth 2,000 × ₹720 = ₹1,440,000, three times as much. Which do you fund?

Answer: The college list. Both halves of the SO pair carry evidence: 9 of 14 interviews behind the strength, a measured pilot behind the opportunity. The WO estimate rests on two claims nobody has checked: that interns want rooms near campus, and that owners who want ten-month tenants will take three-month ones. Even at a one-in-three chance that both hold, its expected value is ₹1,440,000 / 3 = ₹480,000, about the list's, with a far wider spread. Fund the list and test the intern idea cheaply first, as the assumption testing lesson showed: ask 30 owners whether they would take a three-month tenant. The principle: every SWOT item is a claim, and a strategy built on unchecked claims is the list Hill and Westbrook found nobody used.
