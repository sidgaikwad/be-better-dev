The founder comes back from dinner with an investor and opens the Monday meeting with a new idea: "Roost Assured. If the room is not as listed, the family gets the first month's rent back. On every listing, before June." It sounds like the strategy, whose diagnosis is that trust is the obstacle. It is also the kind of idea the video's instructor warns about.

## Why this no is different

In the video's section on new product development, the instructor is candid: sales, marketing, finance and tech all throw ideas at the PM, and saying no, simple as it sounds, is one of the hardest parts of the job, hardest when the idea comes from the CEO, the managing director or the founder. The idea screening lesson recorded the same instructor's admission that ideas from top management often pass screening for political reasons.

The saying no lesson covered the end of that road: the HiPPO, and the override written down with its reason. This lesson covers the stretch before it. Most founder ideas are not yet a yes or a no. They are an untested belief from the person with the most context and the least time. Your job is to turn the belief into something a number can settle, and agree that number with the founder before it arrives.

## From idea to a bar it must clear

First, find the belief: what would have to be true for the idea to work? For Roost Assured: families who hesitate at the ₹27,000 upfront payment would pay if Roost carried the risk, and enough of them would do so to cover the refunds.

Second, price it. Operations reviewed last month's 360 disputes and found 80 where the room was materially different from the listing.

```text
Refund cost
  Claims a month (ops review)               80
  Refund per claim, first month's rent      ₹9,000
  Monthly cost: 80 x ₹9,000                 ₹720,000

Break-even
  Contribution per booking                  ₹525
  Extra bookings needed: 720,000 / 525      1,372 a month
  Booking requests a month                  8,000
  Request-to-booking needed:
    (4,000 + 1,372) / 8,000                 67%, from 50% today
```

The season's OKR aims for 60%. Roost Assured must beat the season's goal by seven points just to pay for itself, before counting that more bookings bring more claims. That is not a no. It is a bar, and a founder can look at a bar and decide.

Third, agree the test and the rule before it runs. Offer the guarantee on half of Pune's booking requests for four weeks in April, and write one line both of you sign: roll it out if the test group reaches 67%; otherwise, not in this form. In the decision rights lesson's terms, the founder stays the Approver and you the Driver. What changes is that the criterion is fixed while nobody knows the answer, so the later conversation is a reading of a number, not a contest of rank.

## When to commit anyway

The founder may know things you do not: what investors will fund, what a rival will launch, how much runway is left. Commit without a fight when the decision is a two-way door, when your evidence is thin, or when the disagreement is about taste. Say your view once, in writing, with the numbers, then give it the full support the one-way doors lesson described. The exception is an idea that deceives or harms users: that gets a written refusal, not a commitment.

One habit helps more than any framework: take the numbers to the founder privately before the meeting. A founder corrected in front of the team defends the idea; a founder shown the arithmetic alone often reshapes it.

## Predict, then verify

The test ends. Request-to-booking was 56% in the test group and 50% in the control. The founder says: "Six points. Ship it everywhere before June." You both signed the 67% rule. What do you do?

Answer: Hold to the rule, and bring a version that could pass it. Six points on 8,000 requests is 480 extra bookings, and 480 x ₹525 = ₹252,000 a month against ₹720,000 in refunds: a loss of ₹468,000 a month. The belief was partly right; the cost is the problem. Since the 80 claims come from owners misdescribing rooms, propose recovering each refund from that owner's payouts, which brings Roost's cost close to zero and keeps most of the ₹252,000 gain. That needs the city leads and owners, so it goes back through the stakeholder map. If the founder still wants the original, record the override and commit. The principle: agree the decision rule before the evidence arrives, so evidence, not rank, settles it.
