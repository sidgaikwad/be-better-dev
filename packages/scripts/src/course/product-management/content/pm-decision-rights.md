In the first meeting, everyone agreed that Featured for large hostels should go from ₹6,000 to ₹7,000 a month next season. The decision took five more weeks. You thought the founder would approve it. The founder thought you had decided and were collecting reactions. The Pune city lead believed she had a veto over Pune prices and asked for more data. The operations lead waited to be asked. Support was on nobody's list. By the time it was settled, renewal notices had gone out at the old price, so the change landed a month late: 60 large hostels x ₹1,000 = ₹60,000 lost on a decision nobody disagreed with.

Disagreement has an exit: someone hears the arguments and decides. Unclear decision rights have none. When nobody knows who decides, everyone's safe move is more data and another meeting, a loop that costs more than any argument.

## RACI and DACI

Two matrices are common. RACI comes from project management and assigns each task four roles: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (answers for the result; exactly one person), Consulted (asked before, two-way) and Informed (told after, one-way). It is built for mapping work across a project plan.

DACI is built for a single decision, which is where PMs get stuck. Atlassian's Team Playbook defines its roles this way:

- **Driver**: gathers the stakeholders and the information, scopes the decision, and gets it made by an agreed date.
- **Approver**: the one person who makes the call. Atlassian's page stresses the "one".
- **Contributors**: people with expertise who give recommendations. They have a voice, not a vote.
- **Informed**: people whose work the decision affects. They are told the outcome.

The Driver is usually the PM, and the Driver is not the Approver. That split is the whole point for someone with responsibility without authority, as the Part 1 lesson of that name put it: you own getting the decision made, not making it.

Here is the DACI that would have saved the five weeks, written on day one:

```text
Decision:  Featured price for large hostels, next season
Options:   A) keep ₹6,000   B) ₹7,000
           C) ₹7,000 for new subscribers, current ones
              held at ₹6,000 for one more season
Decide by: 10 March (renewal notices go out 1 April)

Driver:        PM
Approver:      Founder
Contributors:  Pune, Bengaluru and Hyderabad city leads;
               operations lead; designer (pricing page)
Informed:      Support; engineers; the 60 large-hostel
               subscribers, by notice on 1 April
Evidence:      renewal rate at ₹6,000, rivals' top-slot
               prices by city, bookings per Featured listing
```

Every line answers a question from the hook. The founder knows it is his call, and by when. The city lead knows she is heard but does not hold a veto. The operations lead is asked. Support is told before owners start calling.

## Where DACI breaks

**Two approvers are none.** "The founder and the city lead approve together" means either can reopen the decision after the other signs. If two people each own part of it, split the decision in two.

**The Driver acting as Approver.** A PM who runs the process and then announces the answer has turned Contributors into an audience. Write a recommendation, then let the Approver sign it.

**Contributors counted as votes.** Once people believe the decision needs everyone's agreement, one holdout stalls it. Consensus is a nice outcome, not a requirement.

**The Approver set too high.** If the founder approves every choice, the founder becomes a queue. Push the approval down to the lowest person who owns the consequences: the wording on the pricing page is the designer's call, not the founder's.

## Predict, then verify

The Pune city lead says Pune's owners will not pay ₹7,000 while a rival's top slot costs far less, and adds: "Pune is my market, so Pune's price is my call." The founder replies: "Pricing is mine, everywhere." Of the 60 large-hostel subscribers, 25 are in Pune. Whose name goes in the Approver line?

Answer: Neither alone, because this is two decisions. The founder approves the pricing framework: a list price of ₹7,000, and a rule that a city lead may set a price up to ₹1,000 below list for one season, with a written reason. Within that range the Pune city lead approves Pune's price. The founder's exposure is bounded, at most 25 x ₹1,000 = ₹25,000 a month, and the person closest to Pune's market decides for Pune. Sharing one approval between two people would restart the five-week loop. The principle: every decision gets exactly one Approver, and when two people each need to own part of it, split the decision instead of sharing it.
