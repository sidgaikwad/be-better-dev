In September, Roost's Hyderabad sales team had its best month: 80 new owners signed. Pune signed 120. Both counts measure sales effort, not whether the product works for the owners who signed. The lifecycle lesson found 55% of Hyderabad's owners saying they would list again, against 85% in Pune. Six months on, the cohort data shows what they did.

## The two shapes

A cohort is everyone who started in the same period; track the share still active at each age. Active means the action that carries value: for an owner, answering an enquiry or updating availability during the month, not opening the app.

Owners who listed in September, active in each month after listing:

| Month | Pune, 120 owners | Hyderabad, 80 owners |
| ----- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
| 1     | 85%              | 80%                  |
| 2     | 72%              | 60%                  |
| 3     | 66%              | 45%                  |
| 4     | 63%              | 34%                  |
| 5     | 63%              | 26%                  |
| 6     | 62%              | 20%                  |

Both lose owners early, as every product does. The difference is what happens next. Pune's curve flattens from month 4; Hyderabad's keeps falling. Both cohorts' six months run from October to March, outside the June to August rush, so the season flatters neither.

The clearest reading is the survivor rate: of the owners active one month, the share still active the next.

```text
Pune active owners        102, 86, 79, 76, 75, 74
Survivor rate             84%, 92%, 96%, 99%, 99%

Hyderabad active owners    64, 48, 36, 27, 21, 16
Survivor rate             75%, 75%, 75%, 78%, 76%
```

A curve flattens when the survivor rate climbs toward 100%: the owners who remain have found lasting value and stop leaving. Pune's climbs from 84% to 99%. Hyderabad's sits near 76%, so every month it loses about a quarter of whoever is left. Six more months at that rate keep 0.76 to the power 6, about 19%, of its 16 owners: 16 x 0.193 = 3.1. Pune keeps about 74.

Brian Balfour made this a checkpoint in his 2013 essay on fit: if the curve of the share active over time flattens off, you have probably found fit for some market or audience. The qualifier matters: a flat curve is fit for the cohort that produced it, which is why the table splits by city.

## Why it is harder to fake

- Signups count a single decision, and money buys decisions: a sales push, an ad budget, a launch discount.
- Survey answers are opinions, from the people who chose to reply.
- Retention asks each person to choose again every month with nobody paying them to. A campaign can fill the top of a cohort; it cannot hold up the tail.

Harder is not impossible: pay owners to stay active and the curve lifts only as long as the payments last.

Measure at the rhythm the need recurs. Owners run their business all year, so a monthly curve suits them. A student who has found a room is done until next year, and a monthly student curve falls toward zero whether or not Roost served her well. For students, the same test runs across seasons: do last year's bookers come back, and do they send their friends? Part 5 returns to retention in depth: full cohort tables, activation, and the arithmetic of churn.

## Predict, then verify

Hyderabad starts verifying every charge an owner will ask for, from November's new listings. In early April, November's cohort of 60 owners has four months of data: 49, 41, 36 and 34 active. The city lead wants to hire three salespeople now to sign owners before June; waiting for months 5 and 6 would be too late for the season. Hire now?

Answer: yes. The survivor rates are 41 / 49 = 84%, 36 / 41 = 88% and 34 / 36 = 94%. That is the shape Pune's curve had (84%, 92%, 96%) and nothing like September's flat 75%. The month-4 level, 57%, is not yet the plateau, but fit asks whether the curve bends, and it does, over the same off-season months as September's. The cohort is small: one owner moves the survivor rate by about 3 points, so hire for the season and set a tripwire, such as a month-5 survivor rate below 90%, that pauses the push. Read the shape, whether survivors stop leaving, not the level at one age.
