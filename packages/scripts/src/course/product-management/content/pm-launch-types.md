It is April, and Roost has three releases ready before the June rush. Instalment payments let a student or parent pay the first month's rent and deposit in three parts, through a lending partner. A redesigned owner dashboard shows owners how their listings perform. A fix makes photo uploads 40% faster, and took more engineering weeks than the other two combined. Marketing wants one big launch of all three on June 1.

That request hides two separate decisions: how many people get each release, and how loudly you tell them.

## Three ways to launch

The course's instructor names three approaches.

- **Soft launch**: a limited audience, chosen so you can learn; his example is a software beta. It buys feedback while mistakes are cheap and costs buzz and sample size.
- **Hard launch**: everyone at once, with a full marketing push; his example is Apple launching a device worldwide. It buys one moment of attention and costs the chance to fail quietly.
- **Phased launch**: availability grows by region, segment or user group. It buys control, since each phase teaches the next, and costs time.

The video also calls it a phased launch when an app shows a new feature to 5% of users, then 10, 20 and 30%. That is better named a rollout, an engineering control and the next lesson's subject. A phased launch is a market decision: which cities or segments can buy, and when.

## Launch tiers: how loudly

Launch tiers answer the second question. Jim Semick's version for Pragmatic Institute sizes a launch by its expected business and market impact, not by the engineering effort behind it. Applied to Roost:

| Release             | Tier | What it gets                                      |
| ------------------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Instalment payments | 1    | Campaign, press, trained support, launch-day plan |
| Owner dashboard     | 2    | Email to owners, in-app tour                      |
| Faster photo upload | 3    | A release note                                    |

The photo fix cost the most and gets the least, which is correct: owners will notice faster uploads unprompted, and nobody picks Roost over a broker because of them. Tiers exist because attention is finite, and a company that makes every release tier 1 has trained its audience to ignore tier 1.

## Two dials, not one

Soft, hard and phased mix two dials worth separating: exposure (who can use the release) and announcement (who is told). A soft launch sets both low, a hard launch sets both high, and a phased launch raises them together, one group at a time.

The dials are not equally reversible. Exposure can be turned down: you switch the feature off for a city. An announcement cannot be unsaid, and it fixes the day most people form their first impression. That is the real cost of a hard launch: not the campaign budget, but a first impression that lands on the day the product is least tested. Apple can afford that because a device is tested for months before the event; a startup copying the event for an untried feature takes the risk without the reason.

A soft launch has its own failure: running one without a question. Write down what the phase must show and the number that ends it, such as "100 instalment bookings with payment failures under 2%". Without that, a soft launch is only a delay.

## Predict, then verify

Instalments are built. Plan A: a hard launch on June 1 in all three cities with a ₹1,200,000 campaign. June's forecast is 9,000 bookings; if 25% use instalments, that is 2,250 applications, about 75 a day. The lending partner has never processed more than 40 a day. Plan B: a quiet soft launch in Pune in May (about 600 bookings, so 150 applications), the other cities in June once the exit criteria hold, then the campaign. Marketing warns that parents decide in early June. Which plan?

Answer: Plan B, with the campaign pulled as early as the evidence allows. The unknown is whether the partner's approvals hold at nearly twice their tested volume, with parents paying real deposits in the busiest month. In Pune in May, a defect costs a few families a delay and Roost nothing in public. On June 1 it lands on 75 families a day while the campaign invites more. The principle: raise exposure as evidence arrives, and spend the announcement last, because it is the one step you cannot take back.
