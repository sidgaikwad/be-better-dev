In January 2021, WhatsApp asked its users to accept a new privacy policy, and millions went looking for an alternative. Sensor Tower counted 50.6 million installs of Signal that month, about fifty times its January 2020 figure. Then the surge faded: February to April added only 14 million more, while WhatsApp was installed about 172 million times over the same four months. The video's instructor reads it simply: Signal only matched what WhatsApp did, so it made no dent.

## Minimum viable, minimum desirable

The instructor sets the MVP against an MDP, a minimum desirable product, which he calls "MVP++": it solves the problem in a way people find appealing. His ride-sharing example: the MVP lets riders request rides and drivers accept them, and the MDP adds an intuitive design, real-time GPS tracking, arrival estimates and driver ratings. His rule: where a dominant competitor has set expectations, build the MDP, even if it takes a month or six weeks longer; where the market is new or fragmented, build the MVP.

The idea has several authors: Andrew Chen's minimum desirable product, the simplest experience that proves a satisfying product; Brian de Haaff's minimum lovable product (2013); and Frank Robinson's original MVP from the first lesson, already meant to be big enough for adoption and satisfaction.

## Delta 4

The instructor points to Kunal Shah, founder of FreeCharge and CRED, who explains his Delta 4 framework on Lenny's Podcast (March 2024). Score the efficiency of the old way and the new way out of 10. When the new way wins by 4 or more, three things happen: the change is irreversible, people tolerate occasional failures, and they cannot stop telling others (his unique brag-worthy proposition), so it spreads with little paid acquisition. Below 4, the change is reversible, nobody brags, and nobody forgives a failure. His example is a taxi against Uber, which his host scored 3 and 9.

That second property explains his rule. A rough MVP survives on tolerance, and tolerance comes from the delta. In a new market the alternative scores low, so even a rough product can clear 4. In a crowded one the alternative already scores 7, a rough version scores less, and when it fails you have measured your polish, not your idea.

## Where the rule breaks

"Crowded" is a proxy, and it fails in three ways.

- **It counts products, not alternatives.** As the real competition lesson argued, what matters is what users do today for the job. A category with ten apps is empty for a user who still keeps a notebook.
- **Polish is not the only gap.** By the instructor's own account, Signal matched WhatsApp. For the job "message the people I talk to", an app they are not on scores low however good it is. That is a network effect, and an MDP does not fix it.
- **Testing is not launching.** Drew Houston's slides count dozens of cloud storage companies in 2006, yet Dropbox tested demand with a video. What waited was the public launch, in September 2008; the same slides show why, quoting a rival's user whose documents had become zero-byte files.

Test cheaply in any market; size the launch to the delta.

## Predict, then verify

The rent tool's Wizard of Oz passed: 17 of 50 owners (34%) paid a second month with no phone calls. The engineers have built a lean version: reminders, plus a list where the owner ticks off who has paid. Of the first 100 owners, 88 had managed rent with a notebook and WhatsApp, and 12 used a rent app. The team scores the notebook 3, the rent apps 7, and the lean version 7. The designer wants six more weeks of polish, which she thinks gets it to 8. The founder cites the instructor: rent apps exist, so this market needs an MDP. What do you ship?

Answer: Ship the lean version now, to owners who use a notebook. For them the delta is 4 (7 minus 3), so the change should stick and rough edges will be forgiven. For owners on a rent app the delta is 0, and six weeks of polish makes it 1: still reversible, and not worth making the other 88% wait. Leave app users alone until Roost has a real reason for them to switch, such as tenant records filled from Roost bookings. The principle: size the product to the delta against what the user does today, not to how crowded the category looks.
