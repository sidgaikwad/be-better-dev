Your Friday update lists fourteen things: the visit-date badge shipped, the map pin bug fixed, six owner interviews done, and eleven more. The founder replies with one line: "So are we on track for June?" The update answered everything except the one question its reader had.

## The weekly update

A weekly update exists so that nobody on your stakeholder map needs a meeting to know where things stand. It has three parts: what changed, what we learned, what we need. And it opens with the answer. Barbara Minto, who developed the pyramid principle at McKinsey and published it in _The Pyramid Principle_ (1985), built her method on that order: the main point first, then the reasons that support it. A reader who stops after one line still has the right picture.

```text
Roost weekly update, 9 May               Status: at risk

Bottom line: request-to-booking is 53%, against 60% for
the season. The parent page is our biggest lever.

What changed (start, now, season target)
  Request-to-booking          50%, 53%, 60%
  Median days to payment      5, 4, 2
  Unlisted-charge move-ins    3%, 2.4%, 1%

What we learned
  Requests where a parent opens the parent page book at
  61%; the rest at 49%. Only 30% of requests get a
  parent to the page. (0.30 x 61% + 0.70 x 49% = 53%)

What we need
  Founder: approve adding the parent-page link to the
    booking SMS, by Wednesday.
  Operations: 40 Bengaluru re-visits before 1 June.
```

The metrics are the season's OKRs, not a task list. The outputs and outcomes lesson made the distinction; the update is where it is tested every week. The learning explains the bottom line, and the needs name a person and a date, because an ask without both is a wish.

## The product review

An update informs; a review decides. Run it with four rules. Send or read a short document first, as the writing for readers lesson described. State the decisions needed at the top, each with its Approver from the DACI. Time-box it. End with a decision log: what was decided, who approved it, who acts, and when it will be checked. Invite by the map: manage-closely stakeholders come to the review, keep-informed ones get the update, and the monitor quadrant gets a monthly note.

One rule governs both: no surprises. The founder should never hear bad news for the first time in a review, and never from someone else. The failure has a name among project managers: the watermelon report, green outside and red inside, where status stays green week after week until it turns red all at once.

## Storytelling and the pitch

The video's opening roadmap ends its list of key skills with one it calls underrated: storytelling, which it says you use when pitching an idea, updating stakeholders or interviewing for a job, and which includes delivering a strong elevator pitch. A reliable shape is Minto's own introduction pattern: situation, complication, question, answer. Here is Roost in about thirty seconds:

```text
Situation:    Every summer, about 26,000 students book a
              room on Roost before college starts.
Complication: Their families pay ₹27,000 upfront for a room
              nobody in the family has seen, and fake
              listings have taught them that photos lie.
Question:     So how does a family trust a room in another
              city?
Answer:       Roost visits every property before listing it,
              records every charge, and gives parents a page
              with the visit date and dated photos.
```

The same four beats structure an interview answer. A story is a structure for evidence, not a replacement for it: every line above traces to a number or a strategy line from earlier lessons.

## Predict, then verify

It is Friday, 16 May. The founder meets investors on Monday. You learned on Thursday that instalments will ship two weeks late. Instalments are expected to lift request-to-booking by 4 points, and June brings about 18,000 requests. Do you lead the update with the slip, put it under "what changed", or save it for a call after the investor meeting?

Answer: Lead with it, and call the founder before you send it. Two weeks is about half of June's requests, 9,000, and 4% of 9,000 is 360 bookings, or 360 x ₹720 = ₹259,200 in commission. That is exactly what the founder must know before facing investors, not after. Pair it with the cause, the new date and the ask that would recover part of the loss, such as the parent-page link, which lifts bookings without waiting for instalments. The principle: bad news goes first and early, with a number and an ask, because a stakeholder surprised once starts to discount every green status after it.
