Follow one Roost booking and see who gains from it. The student, and the parent who often pays, get a verified room near campus, booked from home, with no broker's fee. The owner fills a bed for the academic year: about 10 months × ₹9,000 = ₹90,000 of rent. Roost gets 8% of the first month's rent: ₹720.

That is ₹720 / ₹90,000 = 0.8% of the rent the booking produces. And of about 60,000 students active each month, only the roughly 4,000 who book earn Roost anything. Creating value and capturing it are separate problems.

## Creation and capture

_Business Model Generation_ (2010), by Osterwalder and Pigneur, defines a business model as how an organization "creates, delivers, and captures value". The video's instructor calls it the game plan for making money: what you sell, to whom, how you deliver it, and how you profit. The four risks lesson called the capture side viability.

The common models differ in who pays, when, and what they reward:

- **One-time purchase**: pay once, as for a phone. Rewards the next sale, so roadmaps chase new versions.
- **Subscription**: pay each period, as for Netflix. Rewards retention, since the customer decides again every month.
- **Advertising**: free to users, paid by advertisers for attention. Rewards time spent, which can pull against the user.
- **Commission**: a cut of each transaction, as food delivery apps take from restaurants per order. Rewards transactions finished on your platform.
- **Freemium**: a free tier forever plus a paid one, like Spotify's ad-supported tier and Premium. The free tier is a marketing cost that must convert.
- **Licensing**: others pay to build your work into their products, as chipmakers pay Arm royalties on chips using its designs. Rewards ubiquity.
- **Marketplace take rate**: the share of each transaction's value a marketplace keeps. Roost's is 8% of the first month.

The model is a product decision: it decides what the roadmap optimizes. An ad-funded app's PM is pushed toward time in the app; Roost's PM, toward bookings that finish on Roost.

## Roost, worked

Owners pay the commission, and some also buy Featured listings: 300 of the 1,200 owners pay for it, 240 small PGs at ₹2,000 a month and 60 large hostels at ₹6,000.

```text
Commission per booking     8% × ₹9,000                  = ₹720
Commission, average month  4,000 × ₹720                 = ₹2,880,000
Featured, per month        240 × ₹2,000 + 60 × ₹6,000   = ₹840,000
Revenue, average month     ₹2,880,000 + ₹840,000        = ₹3,720,000

Commission, full year      48,000 × ₹720                = ₹34,560,000
June to August, 13 weeks   26,000 × ₹720                = ₹18,720,000
The other 39 weeks         22,000 × ₹720                = ₹15,840,000
```

Commission is 2,880,000 / 3,720,000 = 77% of revenue, so Roost's income is its bookings. More than half of it, 18,720,000 / 34,560,000 = 54%, lands in 13 weeks, while salaries are paid every month. And it is earned once per tenancy: a student who stays three years earns Roost one commission, and a booking that moves to the phone pays nothing.

## Where models break

Charge the side that gets the value and can pay. Owners gain ₹90,000 of rent per booking, while students have free alternatives, so students pay nothing. A commission also tempts both sides to finish the deal off the platform, and the more valuable the match, the stronger the pull. The fix is to make finishing on Roost worth something, like a protected deposit.

## Predict, then verify

The founder proposes replacing commission with a flat ₹2,500 a month per property: "1,200 × ₹2,500 = ₹3,000,000 a month, ₹36,000,000 a year, more than commission's ₹34,560,000, steady all year, with no leakage." 48,000 bookings over 1,200 properties averages 40 a year each, or ₹28,800 of commission. Do you switch?

Answer: No. The average hides the spread. An owner with the average 40 bookings pays ₹28,800 in commission and would pay 12 × ₹2,500 = ₹30,000, about the same. A 6-bed PG with 3 bookings pays 3 × ₹720 = ₹2,160 and would pay ₹30,000, nearly fourteen times more, for a year when it needs Roost mostly in July. Small owners leave, and each is supply students lose. The ₹36,000,000 assumes all 1,200 stay, the one thing a flat fee makes least likely. Commission charges owners when they get a filled bed; fix leakage directly instead. The principle: capture value when it is delivered and in proportion to it, especially on the side you can least afford to lose.
