At a sprint review, Roost's team demos six finished tickets: a share button on the parent page, charge fields on the verification form, faster search results, an Excel export in the owner app, dark mode, and a Diwali banner. The founder asks which of them helps Roost win. The team can answer for two.

## Five layers, five questions

A strategy reaches a sprint through five layers, each answering its own question at its own speed:

| Layer    | Question                                       | Roost                                        | Changes                       |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Vision   | What future are we creating?                   | Every student moves into a room as promised  | Every few years               |
| Strategy | How will we win, and what won't we do?         | Make Roost's visit stand in for the family's | Yearly, or on a new diagnosis |
| Goals    | What result this season, and how will we know? | Request-to-booking from 50% to 60%           | Each season                   |
| Roadmap  | In what order do we attack it?                 | Now: parent page. Next: charges on listings  | Monthly                       |
| Backlog  | What do we build this sprint?                  | Tickets                                      | Every sprint                  |

Read downward, each layer is the "how" of the one above. Read upward, it is the "why" of the one below. Marty Cagan wrote in 2020 that most product organizations he meets do not even have a product strategy. What they usually have is a roadmap with the middle rows missing, answering to nothing.

## Tracing a sprint

The check a PM can run every sprint: for each ticket, write the chain upward and stop at the first break.

```text
Parent-page share button   parent page, request-to-booking,
                           action 3
Charge fields on the form  charges on listings, action 2
Faster search              product health
Owner Excel export         nothing
Dark mode                  nothing
Diwali banner              nothing
```

A ticket that traces to nothing is one of three things: maintenance, which deserves a share of capacity agreed in advance; an opportunity the strategy missed, which goes upward so the strategy changes in the open; or waste, which you cut. What is not allowed is shipping it quietly, because then the backlog sets strategy one ticket at a time.

## Plan, implement, control

The video describes strategic management as a cycle of three stages. Planning sets the vision, analyzes the environment, and formulates objectives and strategy. Implementation allocates resources, communicates the plan, breaks it into tasks and names owners. Control uses KPIs, reviews and feedback to correct course. Its planning example is a tech company that aims to grow market share 20% over three years through product innovation and partnerships.

The loop is right, with two refinements. First, it runs at each layer's speed: sprint reviews correct the backlog, season reviews correct the roadmap, and the strategy moves rarely. Second, control must be able to travel upward. When the roadmap shipped and the goal still missed, the evidence is against the diagnosis, so the strategy should change. That is the video's "strategy is not static", made into a rule. The video's example also lacks a diagnosis: "grow share 20% through innovation and partnerships" is a goal with two nouns attached, so control has nothing to test except the number.

## Predict, then verify

A hostel chain with 40 large properties in Bengaluru offers to buy Featured listings at ₹6,000 a month each, ₹240,000 a month, if Roost builds a bulk-upload tool this sprint and lists all 40 by next week. Visiting 40 properties takes the Bengaluru verifiers about two weeks, at ₹600 a visit. The tool traces to no goal this season. Do you build it?

Answer: build the tool, but put each property live only after it passes its visit, and tell the chain it takes two weeks. ₹240,000 a month is 8% of Roost's ₹2,880,000 monthly commission, a real business reason. The trace check does not forbid work outside the goals; it makes you name the kind of work, and this business commitment fits as long as the guiding policy holds. The shortcut is what would break it: 40 unvisited listings make "every listing visited" false for all 1,240. The visits cost 40 × ₹600 = ₹24,000, a tenth of one month's fees. If the chain walks away over a two-week wait, let it. The principle: work that does not trace is decided in the open, and the guiding policy is the line it may not cross.
