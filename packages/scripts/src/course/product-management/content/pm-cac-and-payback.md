Roost's marketing report for last season ends in bold: acquisition cost ₹286 per student, against ₹525 of contribution per booking, so double the budget. Every number is right, and the conclusion does not follow.

## Blended, paid, and the next rupee

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is sales and marketing spend in a period divided by the new customers won in that period. Count everything that bought them: ads, partner fees, referral rewards, discounts and salaries. For Roost, a new customer is a student making a first booking:

```text
Channel                   First bookings   Spend
Coaching partners             4,000        ₹1,200,000  (₹300 each)
Student ambassadors           4,000        ₹2,000,000  (₹500 each)
Paid Instagram                4,000        ₹3,600,000  (₹900 each)
Marketing team salaries                    ₹1,200,000
Seniors, search, word of mouth  16,000            ₹0
Total                        28,000        ₹8,000,000

Blended CAC = ₹8,000,000 / 28,000 = ₹286
Paid CAC    = ₹8,000,000 / 12,000 = ₹667
```

Blended CAC divides by every new student, including 16,000 nobody paid for. Paid CAC divides only by the students the spend brought in. Neither answers the budget question, which is about the marginal customer: what the next rupee buys. Coaching partners are at their ceiling and ambassadors cost more as they reach less-connected colleges, so a doubled budget goes mostly to Instagram, at ₹900 a student. Blended CAC is the right number for judging last year and the wrong one for deciding next year.

## Payback

Payback is how long a customer takes to return what it cost to win them:

```text
Payback in months = CAC / margin per customer per month
```

Take a hypothetical SaaS product at $50 a month with an 80% gross margin: each account earns $40 a month. If winning one costs $500, payback is 500 / 40 = 12.5 months. David Skok's guideline is under 12 months; he notes that many of the best SaaS companies recover CAC in 5 to 7. Payback matters even when LTV looks healthy: acquisition is paid today and recovered later, so fast growth on a long payback burns cash.

Roost's payback runs in bookings, not months. The first booking returns ₹525 at once, so a coaching partner student (₹300) pays back on the first booking and an ambassador student (₹500) only just does. An Instagram student (₹900) leaves ₹375 unrecovered, and the rest can come only from later years, using the cohort from the lifetime value lesson: 0.35 x ₹525 = ₹184 in year two, 0.25 x ₹525 = ₹131 in year three, 0.12 x ₹525 = ₹63 in year four. That totals ₹378, so the average Instagram student pays back at the end of her degree, and only if her cohort behaves like the last one.

## Buying customers with discounts

The course's instructor explains why platforms pour money into discounts early: once a customer is hooked, the platform earns it back. That is a payback bet, and it holds under three conditions. The customer buys often enough to form a habit. Customers won by a discount behave like the others once it ends. And the margin after the discount is real.

MoviePass never met the third. In August 2017 it cut its price to $9.95 a month for up to one film a day, while paying cinemas the full price of each ticket its members used. It passed three million subscribers by June 2018, and because its costs rose with every film watched, the heaviest users lost it the most. It withdrew the unlimited plan in August 2018 and shut down in September 2019.

Roost fails the first. A student books once a year, and her next booking waits on a move she may never make. There is no habit for a discount to buy.

## Predict, then verify

The growth lead proposes ₹300 off every first booking this season. A May test showed it lifted first bookings by 10%. "₹300 is less than the ₹525 each booking earns, so every discounted booking is still profitable." Ship it?

Answer: No. The discount goes to every first booking, not only to the extra ones. On last season's 28,000, a 10% lift adds 2,800 students, but the discount is paid on all 30,800: ₹9,240,000, or ₹3,300 for each student it added. Each added student is worth ₹903 over her degree, so the discount loses about ₹2,400 on every one. The principle: a discount costs on everyone who takes it and pays back only on the customers it adds, and a purchase made once a year gives it no habit to lock in.
