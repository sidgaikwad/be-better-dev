A private university in Pune writes to Roost. It wants a housing portal under the university's name, showing only verified rooms within 5 km of campus, and it will pay ₹1,200,000 a year. It also wants sign-in through its student system and a monthly report for the dean's office, and the contract must pass a purchase committee that meets monthly. The founder guesses five months to a signature. Roost has never sold to an organization.

## Two kinds of sale

The video draws the line by who the business serves. B2C, business to consumer, sells to individuals, like Flipkart, Zomato or Netflix, where ease of use, convenience and emotional appeal lead. B2B, business to business, sells to companies, like Slack, Salesforce or Zoho, where the product must solve a team's specific problem, and he calls those buyers more rational and value-driven.

Five questions separate them better than adjectives do:

| Question                | B2C                            | B2B                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Who decides             | One person, often in minutes   | Several people, any of whom can say no     |
| Who pays                | The user, or a parent          | A budget holder, from a budget set earlier |
| Who uses it             | The person who chose it        | Employees who may not have chosen it       |
| How long the sale takes | Minutes to days                | Weeks to months                            |
| What success looks like | People come back and pay again | The contract renews and grows              |

For the university: the dean's office decides with finance, IT and legal; the university pays; students use it; the sale takes months; and success is a renewal, granted only if housing complaints fall.

That changes how the PM learns. Roost's 60,000 students a month allow experiments; a B2B product learns from conversations with a few accounts, each worth a lot. The university's ₹1,200,000 equals the commission on 1,200,000 / 720 = about 1,667 bookings.

The "rational buyer" needs a caveat. B2B buyers are people with careers, and whoever picks a tool that fails gets blamed. So they weigh safety, references and how hard leaving would be: caution more than calculation.

## Roles in the B2B ecosystem

The video lists four roles common in software, and what a PM focuses on in each:

1. **ISV, independent software vendor**: builds and sells its own software, like Adobe, Microsoft or a SaaS startup. Focus: features, UX, pricing model, scalability.
2. **MSP, managed service provider**: runs clients' IT on an ongoing basis: cloud infrastructure, security, a helpdesk. Focus: reliability, service level agreements (SLAs), support, integration.
3. **SI, system integrator**: combines other companies' tools into one solution, such as connecting Salesforce to a client's internal systems. Focus, for a PM working with SIs: compatibility, APIs, documentation, deployment.
4. **VAR, value-added reseller**: resells an existing product with services added, such as training, installation or support.

These are roles around a product, not rival kinds of company. Salesforce's partner program uses the same words: ISVs list apps on its AppExchange marketplace, system integrators configure and implement Salesforce for customers, and resellers sell it. For an ISV's PM, the integrator and the reseller are customers too: if your API documentation confuses the integrator, the deal stalls in a meeting you never attend. The video lists SaaS beside these, but SaaS is a delivery model, not a role: an ISV can sell SaaS, and an MSP can run it for you.

## Predict, then verify

The founder wants to accept every term: sign-in through its system (two engineer-weeks), the dean's report (three engineer-weeks), and a new clause that university staff approve every listing before its students see it. Roost's commission is about ₹34,560,000 a year. What do you do?

Answer: Pursue it as a product for colleges, not a project for one. ₹1,200,000 is 1,200,000 / 34,560,000 = 3.5% of a year's commission; ten colleges would be ₹12,000,000, about 35%. So the question is whether the next nine would buy the same thing. Single sign-on and a standard report are what any college will want, so build them once for all. The approval clause puts a gate outside Roost's control in front of every listing, and no other college will run that gate the same way. Negotiate it down to a monthly review, and call five other colleges before writing code. The principle: in B2B, one customer's contract is a request, not a spec; build what the segment will buy.
