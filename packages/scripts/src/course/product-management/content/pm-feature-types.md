Roost's backlog for next season has 31 items. Sorted by score, the top ten are all small improvements to screens students already use: a better filter, a faster photo gallery, a clearer receipt. Together they make a season in which Roost gets slightly better at what it already does, and nothing a competitor would notice. A scoring table cannot see this, because it scores items one at a time. Naming what kind of feature each item is lets you see the mix.

## The six types

The course's instructor sorts features into six types. The examples in the third column are his.

| Type            | What it is for                         | Video's example                              | Roost example                               |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Core            | The product fails its job without it   | Placing a food order                         | Search, booking, paying the deposit         |
| Secondary       | Makes the core easier or nicer         | Scheduling a delivery time                   | A saved shortlist                           |
| Differentiating | The reason to choose you over a rival  | Instagram Stories; Spotify's Discover Weekly | The verification visit; the parent page     |
| UI and UX       | Changes how using it feels             | Dark mode                                    | A map with walking time to campus           |
| Technical       | Invisible, but reliability rests on it | Encryption; load balancing                   | Search that holds up at June's peak         |
| Innovative      | Something not possible before          | AR try-on in IKEA's and Lenskart's apps      | Flagging photos that do not match the visit |

Spotify launched Discover Weekly in July 2015, a playlist of new music made for each listener every Monday. Instagram launched Stories on 2 August 2016: posts that disappear after 24 hours. The instructor calls Discover Weekly the main reason most people choose Spotify. That is his opinion, not a measured fact, but it shows what a differentiator is for.

## The types are not one axis

The six types answer different questions:

1. Core, secondary and differentiating describe value to the customer, relative to the alternatives. They are what you decide with.
2. UI and UX, and technical, describe where the work lives. If Roost's search times out in June, that is a core failure fixed with back-end work.
3. Innovative describes novelty, which is neither good nor bad on its own.

So one feature can carry two labels. Discover Weekly was innovative at launch, differentiating for Spotify, and runs on back-end recommendation work.

Differentiation is also relative, and it expires. Snapchat launched Stories in October 2013. When Instagram copied the format in 2016, its co-founder Kevin Systrom said Snapchat "deserves all the credit." A differentiator that is easy to copy is a lead you hold for a while, not an advantage you keep. That is why Roost's strategy puts its differentiator in the verification visit: an operations team visiting every property is slow and expensive to copy, and a new screen is not.

## A healthy mix

Check a season's plan against the types:

1. Core first, until it works at the load you expect. A differentiator does not help if booking fails.
2. At least one differentiator, taken from the strategy, not the backlog.
3. A standing share of technical work, because nobody requests it until something breaks.
4. Secondary and UI items fill the rest. Innovative items get built when they serve the differentiator.

## Predict, then verify

Roost has five engineers for ten weeks before June: 50 engineer-weeks. Keep 10 for bugs and peak support, which leaves 40. Last June, search timed out in 8% of sessions at peak. The candidates: a search rebuild (technical work on a core feature), 12 weeks; the parent page (a differentiator), 15 weeks; dark mode (UI), 4 weeks; an AI photo checker (innovative), 25 weeks. The founder wants the AI checker for the press story. What goes in?

Answer: The search rebuild, the parent page and dark mode: 12 + 15 + 4 = 31 weeks, with 9 to spare. The AI checker cannot sit beside the search rebuild and the parent page (12 + 15 + 25 = 52, over the 40), and it duplicates work the verification visit already does. Search comes first because failing 8% of peak sessions is a core failure, and in June that costs bookings no differentiator wins back. The principle: fix the core to the load you expect, fund the differentiator your strategy names, and treat innovation as a way to strengthen that differentiator, not a category that deserves budget for its own sake.
