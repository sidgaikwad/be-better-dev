| Where they found a room                | Students |
| -------------------------------------- | -------- |
| A local broker                         | 114      |

Rival apps took 12 of 300, or 4%. The broker and the WhatsApp group took 195, or 65%. The video says value is what makes people choose you over others; for Roost, the others are mostly not products.

## The value proposition canvas

A value proposition is the reason a specific customer picks you over what they would otherwise do. The usual tool is Strategyzer's value proposition canvas, from _Value Proposition Design_ (2014) by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Greg Bernarda and Alan Smith. It expands two blocks of Osterwalder and Pigneur's Business Model Canvas (2010): customer segment and value proposition.

The right side is the customer profile, for one segment:

- **Jobs**: what the customer is trying to get done, whether functional, social or emotional.
- **Pains**: anything that annoys them before, during or after the job, or stops it, including risks.
- **Gains**: outcomes they want. Some are required, expected or desired; some would surprise them.

The left side is the value map: your **products and services**, the **pain relievers** aimed at specific pains, and the **gain creators** aimed at specific gains. Fit is when the left answers what matters most on the right.

```text
Segment: first-year students moving to a new city

Jobs     Secure a safe room near campus before term (functional)
         Show parents the choice was sensible (social)
         Stop dreading a bad surprise on arrival (emotional)
Pains    Fake or already-taken listings; charges that appear
         at move-in; reviews nobody trusts
Gains    The room matches its photos (required); one number
         for the full monthly cost (expected); instalments
         (desired)

Products        Verified listings, booking in the app
Pain relievers  A visit to every property; charges recorded
                at the visit; reviews only from students who
                booked
Gain creators   Book from home before travelling
```

Fill the right side first, from interviews, and rank it; a value map drawn first invents pains to fit its features.

## Beating the real alternative

A student switches only if Roost beats their current option on something they care about. The WhatsApp group is free and instant, and seniors vouch for places, but posts go stale and nobody checks anything. The broker shows the room in person, but charges a fee and pushes the rooms that pay him.

Roost wins where they fail and loses where they are strong. Strategyzer's ad-lib template turns this into one sentence:

```text
Our verified listings help first-year students moving to a new
city who want to secure a room before term by avoiding fake
listings and surprise charges and by letting them book from
home, unlike college WhatsApp groups and local brokers.
```

Strategyzer's own caution: "Fit is a claim until customers confirm it." It names three stages of evidence: customers care about these jobs, pains and gains (problem-solution fit); they use and pay for the offer (product-market fit); it sits inside a profitable, scalable business (business model fit). A canvas of the team's guesses has reached none. Give the parent who pays a separate canvas; their jobs differ.

## Predict, then verify

A rival app launches ₹500 cashback per booking, and the founder wants to match it. The alternative is "Ask a senior": each listing shows reviews from students of the same college who stayed there, and lets you message one. Most of the 81 students lost to the WhatsApp group or a senior said a senior had vouched for their room. Matching the cashback costs 4,000 bookings × ₹500 = ₹2,000,000 a month. Which do you build?

Answer: "Ask a senior", and do not match the cashback. The cashback competes for the 12 students who went to another app, 4% of the losses, and pays ₹500 on every booking that would have happened anyway: ₹2,000,000 is 69% of the ₹2,880,000 monthly commission. The WhatsApp group and seniors took 81, 27%, and their advantage is trust from someone like you, which money does not buy. Same-college reviews from verified students attack it directly. The principle: a value proposition is defined against the alternative customers actually use, so compete on that alternative's strength, not a rival's feature.
