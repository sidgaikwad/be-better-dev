The theme "the price shown is the whole price" is moving from Next to Now. It serves the season's third key result: move-ins that meet a charge not on the listing, from 3% to 1%. The team broke it into six pieces and ranked them by value against effort:

| Rank | Piece                                                         | Needs first      | Weeks |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----- |
| 1    | Charge guarantee: Roost pays any unlisted charge up to ₹3,500 | Recorded charges | 2     |
| 2    | One monthly cost on every listing                             | Recorded charges | 2     |
| 3    | Charges on the parent page                                    | Monthly cost     | 1     |
| 4    | Move-in check: the student confirms charges on day one        | Recorded charges | 2     |
| 5    | Charge fields on the verification form                        | Nothing          | 1     |
| 6    | Owners edit charges, operations approve                       | Charge fields    | 3     |

Cut phases by rank and the guarantee comes first. It cannot be: until charges are recorded, nobody can say which charge was "unlisted". The piece ranked fifth is the root that four others need.

## Phases from the video

The video's overview tells you to map a flexible roadmap divided into phases, MVP, then V1, then V2. Later, moving from MVP to MDP, the instructor splits the roadmap into short term (major feedback, usability issues, top-requested features) and long term (advanced features, scalability, branding), broken into phase one, two and three, with the prioritization framework deciding what goes where. The video's chapter on new product development adds that a roadmap shows milestones, key features and dependencies.

Dependencies are what ranking cannot see. The RICE lesson cited Sean McBride: dependencies can justify going against the score. So the procedure is:

1. Start from the prioritization output, as the video says.
2. Draw each piece's dependencies and pull every root into the phase of the first piece that needs it.
3. Give each phase an exit: the outcome that must move before the next phase starts.
4. Put anything whose value or cost depends on an earlier phase's result after that phase.

For the charges theme:

```text
MVP, Pune, before June
  Charge fields on the verification form
  One monthly cost on each re-visited listing
  Exit: Pune's unlisted-charge move-ins below 1.5%
V1, all three cities, during the season
  Re-visits in Bengaluru and Hyderabad
  Charges on the parent page
  Move-in check
  Exit: 1% across Roost, KR3 met
V2, after August
  Charge guarantee
  Owners edit charges, operations approve
```

A dependency can also be operational. The charge fields help only at properties visited after they ship. Pune has about 400 listed properties; at 50 re-visits a week, operations needs 400 / 50 = 8 weeks and 400 x ₹600 = ₹240,000 to record every Pune charge. So the MVP is one city: the software takes a week, the visits two months.

## Short term and long term

The video sorts phases by kind of work; sort them by what you know instead. Short-term work has known value and known dependencies. Long-term work is a bet whose value or cost depends on what earlier phases learn. The guarantee is two weeks of engineering, not an advanced feature, and it still belongs in V2, because its cost depends on the MVP's result.

Two cautions. Keep V2 as themes: a fully specified V2 is the last lesson's Gantt chart under another name. And Roost is not an MVP; version labels fit a new capability inside a live product, as here, but outside the team, name each phase by its outcome, not by "V1".

## Predict, then verify

The founder wants the guarantee in the MVP, before June, as the season's marketing hook: "No surprise charges, or we pay." About 26,000 bookings land from June to August, the unlisted charge averages about ₹3,500, and Roost earns ₹720 a booking. Where does the guarantee go?

Answer: V2, unless the MVP's exit is met early, in which case it can join V1. At today's 3%, the guarantee pays 26,000 x 3% = 780 families x ₹3,500 = ₹2,730,000, against a season commission of 26,000 x ₹720 = ₹18,720,000: 14.6% of it. At the 1% target it pays 260 x ₹3,500 = ₹910,000, or 4.9%. The same promise costs three times as much before charges are recorded, when Roost cannot even tell a listed charge from an unlisted one. The principle: phase by dependency and evidence, and sequence a bet after the phase whose result sets its cost.
