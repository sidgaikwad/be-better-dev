In the video, a participant mentions a company in the water purifier and water heater business, and the instructor imagines it deciding to sell water bottles. It might be a good idea, but not for a company whose goals it does not serve. A PM, he warns, hears ideas like it from every department.

Screening shrinks that pool to the few ideas worth a concept test. It is cheap, and the instructor says teams often miss it.

## Knock-outs, then scores

Cooper's Stage-Gate splits gate criteria in two. Must-meets are yes-or-no knock-outs, such as whether the project is within the business's strategic mandate: one clear no kills it. Should-meets are scored to rank what survives. Roost's must-meets:

1. It helps students find or live in a room listed on Roost, or helps Roost's owners fill one.
2. Roost can run it with its current teams and at most one partner.

The sales lead's part-time jobs board fails the first: "we have 60,000 students' attention" describes an audience, not a strategy. That is Roost's water bottle. Selling verification to owners who list elsewhere fails it too; Roost-run hostels and the pickup-and-bedding kit fail the second. The four survivors are scored 1 to 5 on weights fixed in advance:

| Idea              | Evidence 35% | Reach 25% | Ease 20% | Revenue 20% | Score |
| ----------------- | ------------ | --------- | -------- | ----------- | ----- |
| Move-in check     | 4            | 4         | 4        | 2           | 3.60  |
| Summer stays      | 3            | 3         | 3        | 5           | 3.40  |
| Video walkthrough | 3            | 4         | 3        | 2           | 3.05  |
| Owner offers      | 2            | 3         | 1        | 2           | 2.05  |

The move-in check scores 0.35 x 4 + 0.25 x 4 + 0.20 x 4 + 0.20 x 2 = 1.40 + 1.00 + 0.80 + 0.40 = 3.60. Its evidence is the unlisted charges students keep reporting, about ₹3,500 at move-in. The top idea gets a concept test this month; anything under 3.0 is dropped.

## The Pugh matrix

Stuart Pugh, an engineering design professor at the University of Strathclyde, presented his concept selection method in Rome in 1981 and set it out in _Total Design_ (1991). It has no weights. Pick one concept as the datum and mark each other concept better (+), same (S) or worse (-) on each criterion. It answers a different question: which version of an idea. For the move-in check, with an in-person verifier as the datum:

```text
                         App checklist   Video call
Trusted by parents             -             S
Cost per check                 +             +
Capacity in June               +             +
Evidence in a dispute          -             S
Pluses and minuses           2 and 2       2 and 0
```

The video call leads, but Pugh did not mean the sums to pick a winner. He meant the matrix to be rerun: make the strongest concept the new datum, fix its minuses by borrowing from the others, and repeat until the options converge. Owner-countersigned photos might fix the checklist's weak evidence.

## Where it breaks

Scores are opinions with decimals. Summer stays trails the move-in check by 0.20, less than one point of disagreement on evidence, which moves a score 0.35. The instructor is candid about the larger failure: ideas from top management often pass untouched, for political reasons, and weak ideas become weak products that fail after the time is spent. The defense is procedure: fix criteria and weights before scoring, write a reason beside every score, and put every idea through the same table, the founder's included.

## Predict, then verify

Summer stays is the founder's idea. He proposes new weights, since "investors ask about revenue": evidence 25%, reach 20%, ease 15%, revenue 40%. Under them summer stays scores 3.80 and the move-in check 3.20. Which goes to concept testing?

Answer: Both, and the original weights stay on record. Summer stays: 0.25 x 3 + 0.20 x 3 + 0.15 x 3 + 0.40 x 5 = 0.75 + 0.60 + 0.45 + 2.00 = 3.80. Move-in check: 1.00 + 0.80 + 0.60 + 0.80 = 3.20. A ranking that flips when one weight moves 20 points is a tie, and a second concept test costs a week, not a build. But weights chosen after seeing the scores write the answer into the question. If revenue matters more this year, change the weights for the next screen, before anyone scores. The principle: set the criteria before seeing the candidates, and let evidence, not argument, break the ties.
