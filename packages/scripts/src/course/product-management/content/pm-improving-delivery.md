Roost handles about 360 disputes a month, each costing about ₹1,000 in support time, and 30% of them end in a cancelled booking. A student who opens one waits a median of 6 days for a decision. When the PM traces a sample of 40, the wait is not in support: agents reply within hours, then forward every refund request to the operations lead, who approves them between property visits. In June the lead is in the field all day.

## Measure, compare, find the bottleneck

Intellipaat's video gives the loop: collect baseline data (CSAT, NPS, retention, repeat purchases), review it on a schedule, compare with your history and competitors, then adjust. Adjusting works only once you find where delivery breaks, and in a service that is usually a queue in front of someone who must approve.

Operational metrics find it where satisfaction scores cannot:

- **First contact resolution (FCR)**: issues resolved on the first contact, divided by all issues.
- **Time to resolution**: from first message to closed, as a median.
- **Contacts per issue**: how often the customer had to come back.
- **Handoffs**: how many people touched the issue.

Roost's disputes, the sample scaled to a month:

```text
Resolved on first contact:   90 of 360       FCR = 25%
Forwarded for approval:      270
Median time to resolution:   6 days (under 1 day if resolved first time)
Contacts per dispute:        3.2
```

Every forwarded dispute waits in one person's queue. That is the bottleneck: the approval step, not the agents, the app or the owners.

## The video's levers

The video lists lean processes and automation, quality control, communication, better tools, and empowered frontline staff. Two carry most of the weight.

**Transparency.** The video's example is order tracking: left the warehouse, in transit, at the local hub, out for delivery. The parcel moves no faster, but each update removes a reason to worry and to write in. Roost's version is a dispute tracker for the student and the parent: received, owner contacted, decision due by a stated date.

**Empowered frontline staff.** The course's instructor says that when you call American Express, the person who answers has the authority to solve your problem instead of promising to escalate or call back, and he credits Amazon with the same. Both are on the record. In 2012 American Express senior vice president Russell Nickson described its Relationship Care approach: scripts dropped, customers decide how long a call lasts, and agents earn variable pay for customer satisfaction on every call. At Amazon, Jeff Bezos's 2012 shareholder letter names a customer service Andon Cord, and Marc Onetto, who had run Amazon's worldwide operations, explained it in McKinsey Quarterly in 2014: an agent who hears about a repeated defect can take the product off the site until it is fixed. He said it eliminated tens of thousands of defects a year.

The Andon Cord shows what most companies miss: empowerment also lets the person who sees a defect stop it recurring. A Roost agent who can refund a surprise charge should also be able to pause the listing that caused it.

## Where it breaks

FCR is easy to game: close the ticket on first contact and the student opens a new one. Count it as resolved only if not reopened within 7 days. And authority without limits becomes leakage, so give it a ceiling and audit a weekly sample.

## Predict, then verify

The proposal: agents may settle a dispute of up to ₹3,500, the average unlisted charge, on first contact, and Roost recovers the money from the owner afterwards. A two-week pilot suggests FCR rises from 25% to 65% and average handling cost falls from ₹1,000 to ₹600. The weekly audit also finds agents approving about 20 claims a month that the lead would have rejected, none recoverable. Do you roll it out?

Answer: Yes, with the ceiling and the audit. Handling falls by 360 x (₹1,000 - ₹600) = ₹144,000 a month. The bad approvals cost 20 x ₹3,500 = ₹70,000. That nets ₹74,000 a month before counting cancellations: if faster decisions save even one in ten of the 108 disputes that end in cancellation, that is about 11 bookings, or 11 x ₹720 = ₹7,920 of commission. Add an Andon rule, three disputes on one property pause the listing, so refunds feed verification instead of repeating. The principle: move the decision to the person facing the customer, bound it with a limit, and send what they see back upstream.
