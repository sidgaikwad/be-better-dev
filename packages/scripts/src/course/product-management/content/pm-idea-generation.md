Roost's last planning meeting produced three ideas in an hour, two of them the founder's, and spent the rest arguing about which was best. If thousands of raw ideas stand behind each success, as the stages lesson showed, three ideas is not a shortlist. It is a bet with poor odds. The course's instructor puts this stage's goal simply: as many ideas as possible, judged later.

## Where ideas come from

The video lists customers, research, competitors, internal teams and trends. At Roost:

- **Customers.** Interview stories and support messages, such as the student charged a ₹4,000 "maintenance" fee on her first day.
- **Data.** Searches that end without an enquiry, listings viewed ten times and never booked.
- **Competitors.** Of first-years who do not book on Roost, 38% use a broker. Whatever the broker does in person, Roost does not do.
- **Trends.** Changes outside the product: a college opening a second campus, a new metro line.
- **Internal teams.** Operations writes about 260 visit notes in a June. Nobody else hears what owners say at the door.

## Three techniques from the video

**Mind maps.** Put the problem in the middle and branch outward to causes, people and moments, with ideas at the tips. The instructor compares it to the detective's board in crime films, photos joined by string; Miro and Lucidchart put it on screen.

**SCAMPER.** Bob Eberle published it in 1971, as imagination games for schools, by compressing the idea-spurring checklist in Alex Osborn's _Applied Imagination_ (1953) into seven prompts: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate, reverse. The video runs it on a reusable water bottle: steel or bamboo instead of plastic, a built-in filter, a collapsible body, a flip top instead of a screw cap and, reversed, a refill subscription at stations. Run on Roost's booking:

```text
Substitute   A verifier's video walkthrough instead of a family visit
Combine      Booking plus a station pickup and a bedding kit
Adapt        Hotel-style short stays for students on internships
Modify       Stretch verification to move-in day: a verifier checks
             the room and has the owner sign off every charge
Other use    Sell verification visits to owners who list elsewhere
Eliminate    The owner: Roost leases and runs its own hostels
Reverse      Students post a budget; owners send offers
```

Seven ideas in ten minutes, several of them bad. That is the point: each prompt forces a new direction.

**Cross-functional workshops.** The video calls these the most common technique: design, marketing and engineering in one room, and at Roost, operations. The mix matters more than the method, because each function has seen different evidence.

## Why quantity first, and where brainstorming fails

Osborn, the advertising executive who named brainstorming, set its rules in _Your Creative Power_ (1948): go for quantity, withhold criticism, welcome wild ideas, combine and improve. Early on nobody can tell which idea is good, good ideas are often combinations of odd ones, and criticism during generation kills the odd ones first.

The group meeting is the weak part. In Taylor, Berry and Block's 1958 study, people who worked alone and then pooled their lists beat real brainstorming groups on both the number and the quality of ideas. Michael Diehl and Wolfgang Stroebe showed in 1987 that the main cause is production blocking: in a group one person talks at a time, and ideas fade while the others wait. The fix: everyone writes alone first, then the group shares and builds.

## Predict, then verify

You run a one-hour workshop for nine: the founder, the sales lead, two operations leads, the designer, three engineers and you. Last quarter's open discussion, led by the founder, produced 14 ideas, 9 of them from the founder and the sales lead. This time he wants to open by presenting his summer stays idea "so everyone has a starting point". How do you run the hour?

Answer: Silent writing first. Everyone writes at least five ideas alone in ten minutes, with the SCAMPER prompts on the wall, and summer stays goes in as one entry among the rest. Nine people writing five each is 45 ideas before anyone speaks, against 14 last time, and the operations leads get the same space as the founder. Opening with his idea would anchor the room on short stays. Then group and combine them; scoring waits for another day. The principle: separate generating from judging, and individuals from the group, so the quantity is real and the loudest voice is not the only source.
