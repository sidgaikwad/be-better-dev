Roost already runs two go-to-market motions and has never named either. A student can find and book a room without speaking to anyone at Roost. An owner is not listed until someone from the operations team has visited, checked the rooms against the photos and walked them through the terms. Both are right, and the reason is arithmetic, not taste.

## Three motions

A motion answers one question: what does the convincing?

| Motion        | What convinces the buyer                          | Fits when                                          |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Sales-led     | A person: demos, negotiation, a contract          | Large deals, several approvers, slow time to value |
| Product-led   | The product, used before paying                   | Small deals, the user decides, value in minutes    |
| Marketing-led | Campaigns and content, then a self-serve purchase | Many buyers, a product hard to try first           |

Product-led growth is the newest label: Blake Bartlett at the venture firm OpenView coined it in 2016, and Wes Bush's 2019 book defined it as the product being the main way to acquire, activate and retain customers. Three questions pick the motion:

1. **Deal size.** A salesperson's hour costs the same whether the deal is ₹700 or ₹700,000.
2. **Buyer complexity.** One person deciding alone, or a committee with a budget cycle and legal review.
3. **Time to value.** How long before a new user sees the benefit unaided. Product-led needs that to be minutes, since nobody is there to explain.

## The arithmetic that picks the motion

```text
Student side
  Revenue per booking   = ₹9,000 rent x 8% = ₹720
  A caller costs ₹30,000 a month and completes 600 calls
  Cost per call         = ₹30,000 / 600 = ₹50
  Calls per booking, crediting the call with a 5% close rate = 20
  Cost per booking      = 20 x ₹50 = ₹1,000, more than the ₹720 it earns

Owner side
  Bookings per property per year   = 4,000 x 12 / 1,200 = 40
  Commission per property per year = 40 x ₹720 = ₹28,800
  One verification and signing visit costs about ₹2,000
  Bookings to repay the visit      = ₹2,000 / ₹720 = 2.8, so the third
```

The owner is a large, trust-heavy deal, so a person is worth sending. The student is a small one, so the product must do the convincing. Motion follows the value of the customer, and a two-sided business can need one per side.

## Product-led needs a product that sells itself

The student side works without a person only because the product shows its value before anyone pays: verified photos, every charge listed, the distance to campus. Where it cannot, product-led stalls. Roost's weak point is the parent, who pays but may never have opened the app.

That is where companies go hybrid. Atlassian is the standard low-touch example: TechCrunch reported its calendar 2011 revenue at $102 million, and co-CEO Scott Farquhar said it had no salespeople. Its fiscal 2025 annual report describes two motions: a self-service "flywheel" for most customers, and a sales-led motion, with field sales and channel partners, to expand its largest accounts. The motion is chosen per customer, not per company.

The common mistake is going product-led with a product that needs two weeks of setup: its free tier fills with signups who never reach value.

## Predict, then verify

In July, 6,000 families a month reach Roost's deposit step and stop; 60,000 more browse. The founder wants callers to phone the parent of every student who views a listing. A two-week pilot against uncalled families finds browsers book at 6% when called and 5% when not, and deposit-step families at 22% when called and 12% when not. Each caller costs ₹30,000 a month and completes 600 calls. What do you fund?

Answer: calls at the deposit step only. For browsers, a caller adds 600 x (6% - 5%) = 6 bookings a month, so each extra booking costs ₹30,000 / 6 = ₹5,000 against ₹720 of revenue. At the deposit step a caller adds 600 x (22% - 12%) = 60 bookings, or ₹500 each, leaving ₹220. Covering all 6,000 families takes 10 callers (₹300,000 a month), who add 600 bookings worth ₹432,000. Compare the 10-point lift, not the 22% total: the 12% would have booked anyway. A human touch belongs only where its cost fits inside the revenue it adds, which makes Roost product-led up to the moment the buyer hesitates over money, and sales-assisted from there.
