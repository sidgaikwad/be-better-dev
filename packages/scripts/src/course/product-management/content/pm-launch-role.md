The course's instructor pauses over a launch plan and asks an awkward question. Look at the work on it: campaigns, sales training, support, operations. The product was built and tested in the steps before, and the beta's bugs are fixed. So what is the product manager for on launch day?

His answer is that the PM becomes the knowledge hub. By launch you know more about this product and its customers than anyone: what beta users loved, the words they used, what confused them, where the product is still weak. That knowledge is useless in your head. It has to reach the people who talk to customers this week. Coordination and timelines he assigns to the project manager, as the lesson on the roles around the PM set out, even though PMs often absorb that work when no project manager exists.

## What each team needs from you

- **Marketing** needs what customers valued, in their own words, and what not to claim. You know which benefit made people act and which feature nobody cared about.
- **Support** needs the known weak spots, the workaround for each, and what to escalate. At Roost the operations team answers support, so they hear this first.
- **Sales** needs the pitch and the objections. Roost has no sales team, but the operations staff who visit owners sell the dashboard on every visit.

## The launch kit

The video lists what a kit contains: sales enablement material, FAQs, demos and videos, support documents, and a messaging playbook so every team says the same thing. The press release and FAQ from the working backwards lesson are its first draft; if the product drifted from them, the kit is where you catch it. An excerpt for Roost's instalments:

```text
LAUNCH KIT: Instalment payments (tier 1)
Message: Move in now. Pay the first month's rent and deposit in 3 parts.
Evidence: 150 families used it in the Pune soft launch. In follow-up
  calls, 22 of 30 said the deposit was the hardest part to arrange.
Say: "approval usually within a few hours", "no extra fee if paid on time"
Do not say: "instant approval", "no checks"
Known weak spots:
  - The payer needs a PAN (India's tax ID); parents abroad cannot apply yet
  - Weekend approvals can take up to 24 hours
Escalate to the PM: a payment taken twice; an approval stuck over 24 hours
Kit owner: PM. Next update: day 3 after launch.
```

## Where the video's view is too narrow

The instructor calls knowledge transfer "the only role practically" during launch. Two more things belong to the PM, because nobody else holds the evidence for them. The first is the go or no-go call against criteria written in advance, the exit criteria and halt rules from the last two lessons. The second is the definition of success: which metrics, which targets, and instrumentation live before launch day, because you cannot measure afterwards what you did not record.

The run-of-show, the timeline and the daily check-ins are coordination. At Roost there is no project manager, so the PM does them too, and should notice when they crowd out the hub work, since nobody else can do that part. In a larger company a product marketing manager owns messaging and enablement, and the PM's job is to feed that person evidence.

## Predict, then verify

Five days before the June campaign, the marketing lead's ad reads "Instant approval. Pay rent in 3 easy parts." A small ad test showed "instant" earns 30% more clicks. The soft launch data says median approval takes 4 hours and 18% of applications are declined. The campaign forecast is 1,500 applications. Do you let the line run?

Answer: no. Rewrite it with the marketing lead into something true that keeps the pull, such as "Approval in hours. Pay the deposit in 3 parts." Run the numbers on "instant": 1,500 x 18% = 270 families declined after being promised approval, and 1,230 approved families waiting hours for something promised instantly. If one in four of those writes in, that is about 308 more contacts, so roughly 580 support conversations caused by one word, landing on the operations team in its busiest month. The extra clicks were borrowed against a promise the product breaks. The PM does not write the ad but owns the facts in it: the hub's job is to make every team's words match what the product does.
