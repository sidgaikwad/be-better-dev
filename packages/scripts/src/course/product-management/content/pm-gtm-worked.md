The video's worked example is a smart thermostat launch that fills in every component in a line: homeowners aged 30 to 50 in regions with extreme temperatures, "save up to 30% on your energy bills", Amazon and Best Buy, Facebook and Google ads, Nest and Ecobee as rivals. Almost nothing in it could turn out wrong, because almost nothing in it is a number. Here is the same exercise for Roost entering Chennai, with every number derived and checkable.

## Market, segment and message

The video's Priya picks cities the way, it says, Starbucks picks store sites: where the need is sharpest. Chennai passes Roost's three tests: many students arriving without college housing, a broker-run room market, and a Bengaluru playbook that transfers.

```text
Market sizing, Chennai launch zone (Roost's estimates)
First-year intake, 30 colleges in the zone   50,000
From outside Chennai, 50%                    25,000
Without a college hostel seat, 60%           15,000 need a room
First-season share, 10%                       1,500 bookings
```

The ICP is Pune's with the city swapped: a first-year student from elsewhere, searching in May and June, a parent paying. The positioning statement: for first-year students moving to Chennai whose parents must trust a room they have not seen, Roost is a housing marketplace that lists only properties its team has visited; unlike brokers and WhatsApp groups, it shows checked photos and every charge before the deposit. The motion is Roost's usual: owners signed in person, students served by the product.

## Supply first

As the marketplace economics lesson showed, a launch is gated by the scarcer side, which for Roost is verified rooms.

```text
Bookings per new property, first season (other cities)    5
Verified properties needed = 1,500 / 5                  300
Visits needed if 25% fail = 300 / 0.75                  400
Working days, April 1 to May 15, about                   30
Visits per day = 400 / 30                              13.3
Verifiers at 4 visits a day = 13.3 / 4 = 3.3, so          4
```

Verifiers are hired locally, not borrowed from Bengaluru, because many owners will prefer to negotiate in Tamil.

## Channels, budget and break-even

```text
Channel               Bookings   Per booking   Cost
Coaching partners        300        ₹300       ₹90,000
Student ambassadors      600        ₹400       ₹240,000
College search pages     300        ₹150       ₹45,000
Organic and referral     300          ₹0       ₹0
Demand total           1,500        ₹250       ₹375,000

Verifiers, 4 x ₹35,000 x 2 months              ₹280,000
Visit travel, 400 x ₹150                       ₹60,000
Tamil-speaking support, 3 months x ₹30,000     ₹90,000
Total cost                                     ₹805,000

Average rent, sample of 50 PGs in the zone     ₹8,500
Revenue = 1,500 x ₹8,500 x 8% = 1,500 x ₹680   ₹1,020,000
Contribution = ₹1,020,000 - ₹805,000           ₹215,000
```

Channel costs come from the channels lesson's Pune tests, with ambassadors budgeted at the ₹400 pass line rather than Pune's ₹364, because a new city starts colder. The targets: 300 verified properties by May 15 (leading), 1,500 bookings by August 31 and under ₹400 a booking per channel (lagging). The main risk carries a trigger: fewer than 150 verified properties by April 30 means two more verifiers that week.

## Measured against the thermostat

The thermostat plan's best row is its risk: backup hardware suppliers, the thing that actually stops hardware launches. Its weakness is that nothing connects the rows. Two segments (tech-savvy early adopters, environmentally conscious families) share one message, there is no sales target, and the ads carry no cost per sale. Roost's plan is barely longer, but each row is computed from the one above, so when one number moves you know which others follow.

## Predict, then verify

Despite two extra verifiers hired at the April 30 trigger, only 220 properties are verified by May 15. The city lead proposes listing 80 more as "verification pending" so the 1,500 target stands. What do you do?

Answer: refuse, and cut the target to what verified supply carries: 220 x 5 = 1,100 bookings, earning 1,100 x ₹680 = ₹748,000. Demand spend falls to 1,100 x ₹250 = ₹275,000. Other costs stay at plan, ₹430,000, plus about ₹35,000 for the two extra verifiers' final two weeks, so total cost is ₹740,000 and the season still clears ₹8,000. The plan bends. A pending listing breaks the positioning instead: the "unlike brokers" clause is why a parent pays a deposit through Roost, and one bad room in a new city travels through the same WhatsApp groups Roost uses as a channel. The principle: the constrained side of a marketplace sets the target, and you never trade away the attribute your positioning rests on to hit a number.
