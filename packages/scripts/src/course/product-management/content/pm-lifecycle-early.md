On April 23, 1985, Coca-Cola replaced a formula that had not changed in 99 years with a sweeter one. Nearly 200,000 consumers had tasted it first, and the new formula won. The backlash was immediate. On July 11 the original came back as Coca-Cola Classic, 79 days later (7 days left in April, then 31, 30 and 11).

The course's instructor tells New Coke as the classic introduction-stage failure. Strictly, it was not one: it was a change to one of the most mature products in the world, and the taste tests missed what Coca-Cola later called the bond people felt with their Coke. His lesson survives the correction, though. The feedback that decides a launch arrives after the launch, and a team that hears it and acts within weeks survives its mistake, as Coca-Cola did.

Theodore Levitt made the product lifecycle famous in Harvard Business Review in 1965, with four stages: market development (now usually called introduction), growth, maturity and decline. This lesson covers the first two, and what changes between them is the question the PM answers.

## Introduction: does it work, and for whom?

Few people know the product exists, fit is unproven, and scope should stay loose enough to change weekly. Marketing buys awareness rather than conversion, and the arithmetic shows why. Roost's forecast for its first season in Hyderabad, its newest city:

```text
Students needing a room near covered campuses   30,000
Aware of Roost, 10%                              3,000
Of those, booked through Roost, 20%                600 bookings

Double conversion to 40%:   3,000 × 0.40 = 1,200
Double awareness to 20%:    6,000 × 0.20 = 1,200
```

Both levers double bookings, but only one has room to keep going: awareness at 10% can double three times before it reaches 80%, and conversion at 20% cannot. So early money goes to being known, while the product team works on why the other 80% did not book.

Pricing is decided here too. Joel Dean named the two classic launch strategies in 1950. **Price skimming** starts high, for the buyers who want the product most, and steps down to reach the next segment. **Penetration pricing** starts low to win share fast, and pays when being big makes the product better. Roost's student side is free, a penetration choice: every student makes Roost worth more to owners. The pricing section returns to the choice.

The instructor's warning here matters most: many products die because they stay too long without traction. Write the exit test before launch ("by August 31, 600 bookings, and 70% of owners say they will list again"), or the team will keep finding reasons for one more season.

## Growth: how do we scale it?

Once people arrive on their own, sales rise fast and rivals notice. The PM's work changes:

- Scale the team and the channels together. In a growing Roost city the bottleneck is verification visits, not code.
- Choose segments deliberately, starting with the users who get the most value.
- Sharpen the value proposition as competitors copy the obvious features.

The instructor's example is Notion, which he says began with productivity enthusiasts and expanded to students, startups and enterprises, refining features for each segment in turn. The record fits: Notion 2.0 launched on Product Hunt, the enthusiasts' home ground, in March 2018, and in May 2020 Notion made its Personal plan free while Team and Enterprise plans carried what companies pay for. One product, packaged per segment.

## Predict, then verify

Hyderabad's first season ends with 640 bookings against a target of 600. But only 55% of its owners say they will list again, against 85% in Pune, and 18% of its bookings came with a complaint about charges the listing did not show, against 5% in Pune. The founder says Hyderabad has reached growth and wants to buy 600 more bookings next season with paid ads at ₹900 each, ₹540,000. Do you agree?

Answer: No. Hyderabad is still in introduction, whatever the booking count says. The stage is set by evidence of fit, and Hyderabad fails its own exit test: 55% of owners will list again, not 70%, and students are meeting the hidden charges Roost exists to prevent. Each paid booking also loses ₹180, since ₹900 of ads earns ₹720 of commission, and only repeat business recovers that; unhappy customers do not repeat. Spend the off-season verifying charges; scale when relisting and complaints near Pune's. The principle: growth spending amplifies whatever the product already is, so earn fit before paying for reach.
